# Planning workspaces help you do and decide

Decided with the owner on 2026-10-20, after a review of their real planning use. Amends ADR 0020 (documents and Things),
and replaces the "three unrelated workspaces" test (AGENTS.md) as the gate for planning features.

The owner's 420 messages in 33 planning sessions (7 to 20 October; mostly mountain biking, then the garage and their
business) came down to a few jobs: doing a hands-on task live from photos, choosing what to buy within a budget,
carrying a project over days and sessions, keeping a guide they come back to, and coaching. What got in the way was
how answers read (too long, too hard for a beginner, assuming kit, products that don't exist, diagrams they couldn't
read) and every new session knowing nothing of the last. Meanwhile planning grew views around kit records, often in
the code workspace's shape: Planning Home had six shapes in ten days, and its views, price checks, floor plans and a
model-guessed status line shipped on one evening, before any was tried. A plan was a scatter of context lines; there
was no project and no decision to hold one.

So:

- **One engine, different on top.** Planning and code workspaces share sessions, the frame, the Reply box, Talk,
  skills and model choice. They differ on purpose in what models are told (planning has its own conduct: brief,
  tables first, one step at a time when hands-on, asking instead of assuming, real products with checked links,
  checking what's saved, honest about what needs a pro), in what they keep, and in what Home shows.
- **What planning keeps:** Things, where a part holds what's on it and what's **upcoming** next; **projects** (a goal,
  steps marked *you can do this*, *needs a pro* or *decided*, decisions with their options, pick and why, and what's
  next), files in the context folder like documents and Things, with undo; **documents only for reference** the owner
  would look up, one screen long; and a short **note** each session leaves (done, decided, next), which the next
  session in the workspace reads. The context file keeps Facts; its Plans lines move into projects.
- **Buying starts from the part:** Find a replacement fills in the part and a budget to tap, and answers with real
  options side by side, a pick becoming the upcoming part.
- **Home shows the main subject, then the projects:** the bike part by part, or the space's floor plan, and below
  them each project's next step.
- **A planning feature earns its place** by taking friction out of one of those jobs, living where the job happens,
  and replacing something. Step by step, the planning status line, the wanted list's price checks and blocked shops,
  and the other Home views go.

## Considered options

- **Fix each planning feature in turn.** Rejected: they answered jobs the owner doesn't bring, and each fix kept a
  shape the owner never asked for.
- **Separate apps for planning and code.** Rejected: the session engine, the frame and Talk serve both well; the
  difference is in the brief, the records and Home, not the plumbing.
- **Keep plans as context lines and lean on the model.** Rejected: plans scattered over 250-character lines are why
  nothing felt joined up and why sessions forgot each other.
- **A special screen for hands-on help.** Not yet: the conduct comes first, and a screen only if a week of use asks.

## Consequences

- **Fewer, deeper features.** Parked to Later: Ideas, the journey book, Pin to Home, Teach, sub-workspaces, groups,
  Tidy and the drawing/photo switch, each back only when real use asks.
- **The owner's documents and Plans lines move**, each move proposed and ticked by the owner, saved with undo.
- **Models' mistakes become eval scenarios**, so a fixed one (new pedals made a new Thing; a saved tyre pressure asked
  again) can't come back unseen.
- **One more model call per session** (its note), against one fewer after every planning turn (the status line).
